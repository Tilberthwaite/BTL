What the sold price data actually shows — and what it doesn’t

12,246 Barrow-in-Furness sold price transactions from 2015 to 2026 underpin this plan. Before using the data, it’s important to state one honest limitation clearly: the dataset has no bedroom count. We cannot directly filter for 4-bed properties. Everything below is based on price bands and sq ft proxies where available, with Gareth’s own completed deal as the primary verification.

The sq ft data matters: 460 properties have recorded floor areas. Of the 4-bed proxies (900–1,300 sq ft), those selling in the £130k–£160k band have a median of £137,500. This supports £130k–£140k as a realistic post-refurb range for refurbished 4-beds — but only on suitable streets.

What the data cannot confirm

No bedroom count in dataset. The £100k–£180k terraced band includes both 3-bed and 4-bed properties. Some of those 1,547 sales above £130k will be well-presented 3-beds, not 4-beds. We cannot separate them without inspecting individual addresses.

Street matters enormously. Averages hide huge variation. Oxford Street terraced averages £154k recently. Some other streets cap out at £90k–£110k regardless of condition. A property on the wrong street will not achieve the planning figure however good the refurb is.

Surveyor risk — when the lender’s valuer comes in low

Lenders send their own RICS surveyors whose job is conservative valuation — they must be able to justify lending against what the property could recover in a forced sale. They commonly value 8–15% below market value. This affects how much capital the BRRR returns, but has a counterintuitive effect on cashflow.

The break point at £130k: Surveyor must not go below 86.3% to return all capital. At 85% they’d come in at £110,500 and only £1,227 would be stuck — the property is still profitable, just that small amount can’t be recycled. This risk is manageable but tight. Target properties where the street evidence supports £140k+, so even a conservative surveyor comes in above £112k.

The counterintuitive twist

Lower surveyor valuation = better monthly cashflow. If the surveyor comes in at 85% (£110,500), the mortgage is only £82,875 instead of £97,500 — saving £65/month. The property cash flows at £370/month instead of £306/month. The only downside is a small amount of capital stuck in the property that can’t be recycled immediately. The property is still profitable either way.

Tax on disposal — know before you sell

The plan is to hold, not sell. Refinancing doesn’t trigger tax — only an actual sale does. When a Ltd company sells, the profit is subject to Corporation Tax (19%), not Capital Gains Tax (which applies to individuals).

Ltd company vs personal on disposal: ~26% effective vs ~18% personal for a basic rate taxpayer. But the company saves far more during the holding period through full mortgage interest deductibility. Net position strongly favours the company overall. Get accountancy advice before any planned sale — there may be planning options.

Selling costs to budget: estate agent 1.5–2.5%, solicitor ~£1,500, EPC if expired, mortgage redemption fee if in fixed term. Total ~3–4% of sale price. Factor in before deciding to sell.
