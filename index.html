Executive summary
The opportunity

Barrow-in-Furness offers an unusual combination: strong, stable, well-paid employment (anchored by BAE Systems' nuclear submarine programme — £31bn Dreadnought contracts through the 2030s plus AUKUS) and residential property prices well below the national average. Four-bedroom homes regularly appear at auction for £45,000–£55,000 hammer price; post-refurbishment they achieve valuations of £120,000–£140,000.

The BRRR model — Buy at auction, Refurbish, Refinance, Rent — allows a single initial investment to fund multiple properties in sequence. The 75% LTV mortgage on a £130,000 valuation returns £97,500, repaying the £75,000 all-in cost and generating a £22,500 surplus that funds the next acquisition. The model becomes self-sustaining within 12–18 months.

The financials (verified)

At the current mortgage rate of 5.25% IO (April 2026, Ltd company 5-year fix), the mortgage on a £97,500 loan costs £426/month — 47% of the £900 rent from a 4-bed property. Net cash flow in Year 1 is £249/month per property. This is correct and expected for interest-only BTL.

The model works because the mortgage is fixed while rent grows. ONS data shows Westmorland & Furness rents rose 7.1% in the 12 months to December 2025. Using a conservative 5%/year, the same £249/month net in Year 1 becomes £443/month by Year 5 from the same fixed mortgage.

Rising property prices help, not hurt: At 3%/year growth (Barrow 5yr average: 14.4% per Land Registry), the 75% LTV drawdown on later properties grows proportionally — surpluses increase each year.
